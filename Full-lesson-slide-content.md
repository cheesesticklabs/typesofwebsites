# Why Websites Exist
## How Design Demonstrates Purpose

Full lesson slide-content draft for review. This is proposed copy for a 32-slide sequence, including teaching, guided practice, checkpoints, the 20-minute investigation, and the exit ticket. Student-facing copy appears under **On slide**. Visual suggestions and teacher notes stay off the projected slide. The supplied PDF is preserved unchanged.

### Pacing for a 60-minute period

| Minutes | Lesson segment |
|---|---|
| 0–6 | Do Now and brief debrief |
| 6–8 | Goal, CSSA recap, and purpose |
| 8–23 | Three guided examples and investigation launch |
| 23–43 | Group investigation: full 20 minutes |
| 43–51 | Eight group share-outs, about one minute each |
| 51–53 | Big Idea and transfer |
| 53–58 | Independent exit ticket and submission |
| 58–60 | Buffer |

Teacher note: Checkpoint slides are quick transitions, not additional timed activities. Slide 25 stays projected for the full investigation. The launch slides explain the task before that timer begins. The guided section is brisk: approximately four minutes per website type and three minutes to launch the investigation.

## Slide 3 — CSSA: What are we looking at?

**On slide**

**Content:** What the site includes.

**Structure:** How it is organized.

**Style:** How it looks.

**Accessibility:** How people with different needs can access and use it.

**Visual:** Four labeled examples: a paragraph and image; a navigation menu; text and button colors; a captions control.

**Teacher note:** This is a recap of Monday. A feature can involve more than one category. Students should explain the aspect they are discussing.

## Slide 4 — Purpose

**On slide**

**What does this website help or encourage visitors to do?**

Purpose helps explain why designers choose particular content, organization, appearance, and features.

**Teacher note:** Connect back to the Do Now visitor task: finding the care guide gave students a reason for their design decisions.

## Slide 8 — Design that helps us understand

**On slide**

**Content:** Explanations, diagrams, and facts.

**Structure:** Headings and links that organize a topic.

**Style:** Readable text and a clear heading hierarchy.

**Accessibility:** Image descriptions and text that can be enlarged.

**Visual:** World Atlas volcano article with callouts to its explanations, contents menu, headings, and larger-text button.

**Teacher note:** Connect each feature to learning or finding an answer. Do not imply that every information website has exactly these features.

## Slide 9 — Explain the connection

**On slide**

**Feature:** The “On this page” menu.

**CSSA:** Structure.

**Effect:** Visitors can jump to the explanation they need.

**Purpose:** It helps them find information.

**Visual:** Enlarge the World Atlas contents menu. Highlight it as you model.

**Teacher note:** Model one numbered annotation and its comment in the lab. The explanation should connect the feature to a visitor action and then to purpose.

## Slide 22 — Part 1: Explain the design

**On slide**

**Agree on the site’s main purpose.**

Find one piece of supporting evidence from each category:

**Content · Structure · Style · Accessibility**

Everyone makes **four annotations**.

For each: select the category, identify the feature, and explain how it supports the purpose.

**Teacher note:** The group can agree on the evidence together. Every student needs their own annotated record and explanations.

## Slide 23 — Part 2: Improve the website

**On slide**

**Your group has been hired to improve this site.**

Choose one specific change that would help it accomplish its purpose.

Everyone adds a fifth annotation: **“Our improvement.”**

What would you change? Which CSSA category?

What would it help visitors do, and why would that support the purpose?

**Teacher note:** Students attach the proposal to the relevant part of the page. They are writing a design proposal, not editing the site. If needed, offer a spoken example: adding a difficulty label beside a puzzle could help visitors choose a game they would enjoy.

## Slide 24 — Get ready to defend your choice

**On slide**

**Which ONE existing feature is your group’s strongest evidence?**

Compare your four examples.

Choose the most convincing one and explain why.

Be ready to show it, then describe your proposed improvement.

**Teacher note:** Strongest existing evidence and proposed improvement are separate parts of the share-out. Students answer three questions: identify the purpose; justify the strongest evidence by comparing it with another example; explain the proposed improvement and why that CSSA category needs attention.

## Slide 25 — Your 20-minute work plan

**On slide**

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 3 minutes | Explore and agree on the purpose. |
| 9 minutes | Make four explained CSSA annotations. |
| 5 minutes | Propose and annotate one improvement. |

**Everyone finishes with:**

Four evidence annotations + one improvement annotation + three written answers.

**Teacher note:** Leave this slide projected during the full 20-minute work period. Check progress at minutes 3, 12, and 17. The share-out follows this work period.
