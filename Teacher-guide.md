# Why Websites Exist — pre-observation guide

**Robert Pino · Cheesestick Labs**

**Learning target:** Students identify a website’s primary purpose and explain how Content, Structure, Style, or Accessibility supports that purpose using annotated evidence.

**Subtitle:** How Design Demonstrates Purpose

**Essential question:** How does a website’s purpose influence its design?

## The explanation to give in the meeting

Students begin by retrieving CSSA vocabulary and redesigning a simple Dog Zone homepage using the supplied dog photo. A brief Purpose page introduces information, entertainment, and sales before the guided cycles. I model how to connect a specific feature to a website’s purpose. During three short guided cycles, students annotate a feature, explain it, and discuss their reasoning. Tables then investigate different fictional websites, while each student produces individual evidence. The Big Idea synthesis and independent exit ticket check whether students can explain how the same feature serves different goals.

## Pacing for a 60-minute period

| Minutes | Learning sequence | Evidence and teacher action |
|---|---|---|
| 0–9 | Do Now: CSSA matching, sample-site changes, explanation | Check vocabulary; ask how a change helps a visitor. |
| 9–24 | Three guided cycles, 5 minutes each | About 2 min teaching, 2 min practice, 1 min share. Fold the purpose introduction into the first cycle. |
| 24–39 | Table investigation, individual annotations | 3 min table claim, 8 min annotation, 4 min explanation. Circulate and ask students to open a numbered annotation. |
| 39–48 | Table sharing and Big Idea comparison | Compare claims and ask for evidence supporting an alternative interpretation. |
| 48–51 | Independent exit ticket and hand-in | Wikipedia/Amazon search-bar comparison. Download and attach work as directed. |
| 51–60 | Flexible margin | Use across the lesson for entry, transitions, slower work, and hand-in. Protect the exit ticket. |

The source plan labeled the guided section 15 minutes but listed 19 minutes of substeps. This version keeps it at 15. If time runs short, reduce the number of table share-outs and accept a concise spoken explanation before the written response. Keep the independent exit ticket.

## Success criteria and monitoring

- A defensible primary purpose supported by specific features.
- Four primary-purpose annotations, one per CSSA category, with explanatory comments. Then at least one additional annotated piece of evidence for a possible secondary purpose.
- A written claim that refers to an annotation number and explains how the feature supports the purpose.
- A secondary purpose supported by different evidence, or a reason the evidence is insufficient.

Guided tool sequence: Information uses Highlight, Entertainment uses Connect, and Sales uses Arrow. Use Escape to cancel a picked-up tool. A completed annotation releases the tool and opens its comment. Pins also move with the keyboard arrow keys when focused.

**Feedback prompts:** “What does the visitor do because of that feature?” “Which CSSA choice are you describing?” “How does that action support your claim?” “Could that same feature support another goal?”

**Fast check:** If a student names a feature but gives no connection, ask for “This helps visitors ___ because ___.” If a student calls every product mention sales, compare the whole site’s dominant goal with its additional goal. Accept CSSA overlap when the explanation is sound, especially contrast as Style and Accessibility.

These are fictional teaching models, not live replicas. The site appears inside a browser frame, with actual HTML elements. Highlight previews the element under the pointer and marks it on click. Arrow and Connect use two clicks or a drag to place endpoints. Each creates a numbered, draggable pin linked to a comment card. Clicking either selects the matching pin/card. The classroom pages do not perform real purchases or video playback. The simplified models make the evidence available without external logins or changing websites. Students should evaluate design evidence rather than infer intent from a name alone.

## Access and participation

English/Spanish directions, sentence frames, a glossary that grows by visited page, light/dark themes, and click/keyboard annotation controls support access. Teacher circulation and brief partner discussion support students before individual writing. The Do Now intentionally includes low-contrast text as a design problem; students can immediately change it. The main lab controls use the readable theme.

Students discuss as a table but each creates their own annotations and response. Do not infer individual understanding from the table’s spokesperson alone. Invite a student to show an annotation and explain the visitor action it supports.

## Exit ticket and interpretation

**Prompt:** Wikipedia and Amazon both use search bars. How does the same feature help each website achieve a different purpose?

**Strong response:** Wikipedia’s search helps visitors find articles and learn about a topic, supporting information. Amazon’s search helps visitors find and compare products they may buy, supporting sales.

**3 — Secure:** Explains both purposes and connects search to each goal. **2 — Developing:** Identifies both purposes but explains only one connection. **1 — Beginning:** Names search or a purpose without a clear connection. **0 — No evidence yet:** Blank or unrelated. Use this as a proposed formative check, not an adopted school grading policy.
